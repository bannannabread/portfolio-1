# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

delegated: Astro. Recommended for a visual, design-forward portfolio because it ships zero JS by default (fast first paint matters when a recruiter is skimming quickly), has first-class image optimization, and still allows componentized layout reuse across project pages without requiring a client-side framework.

## Users

Recruiters and hiring managers evaluating the site owner as a design candidate. Their job: quickly assess skills and quality of past work to decide whether to extend an interview invitation.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio site showcasing design work, built to convince a recruiter or hiring manager, after reviewing the owner's skills and work, to offer an interview.

## Positioning

The site itself functions as a design work sample, not just a container that describes one elsewhere (e.g. a PDF resume or LinkedIn). Its own visual and interaction quality is evidence of the owner's craft.

## Operating Context

Reviewed by recruiters/hiring managers during candidate screening — likely a fast, skimming first pass, on both desktop and mobile, often alongside a resume or application.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Some real projects are ready to include now; other project slots still need content and will use placeholders until the owner supplies real details.
- Placeholder project entries must be visually honest about being placeholders — not fabricated case studies, fake metrics, or invented client names.

## Brand Commitments

Name: Megan. Positioning line: "Product designer who engineers" — leads with the hybrid design+engineering identity rather than a generic title.

Visual world is pinned by the user, not open for a fresh direction roll. After reviewing more reference pages, the user refined this to: closest in overall design direction to Rachel Chen's and Sanvithi's portfolios ("classy"), while staying warm in palette (the quality pulled from Selina Turfus). Concretely:

- **Classy register (Rachel Chen + Sanvithi):** elegant serif display headlines; italic serif for first-person project captions ("I designed...", "I rebuilt..."); small-caps / sentence-case metadata tags under each project (role, domain, status — e.g. "Enterprise / SaaS · Interaction Design · Shipped"); generous whitespace on a light neutral ground; a footer credit line in the vein of "Designed + coded by Megan" with contact/social links as understated pill buttons or plain text links. Sparing, occasional hand-touches (a single script/handwritten accent word, never the dominant motif) are welcome but must stay minor.
- **Warmth (Selina Turfus, filtered through Sanvithi's card pattern):** each project card sits inside a soft gradient field behind a floating device mockup with a soft reflection — Sanvithi already does this in cool tones (blue, sage green); render the equivalent in warm tones (coral, apricot, warm peach, dusty rose) so the gradient-card device is kept but the palette shifts warm. Warm color stays confined to these card/section fields as a deliberate accent, not spread across the whole page.
- **Explicitly de-emphasized:** Nicole Roberts's teal palette and metric-badge-heavy card style, and Selina's literal large blended "aura" hero blob — those informed the search but are not the pinned look.

Structural cues to carry into the build:
- Rachel Chen's case study format: sticky in-page section nav (Challenge/Problem/Solution/Outcome), a role/timeline/team/skills metadata row under the title, small-caps eyebrow labels, playful small illustrative accent shapes used sparingly against an otherwise clean layout.
- Sanvithi's project card format: gradient-field card, floating device mockup with reflection, italic serif outcome caption, small-caps meta tag row beneath.

## Evidence on Hand

Some real project content exists but specifics have not yet been provided to this assistant. Remaining project slots have no real content yet — do not invent case studies, testimonials, clients, or outcomes for them.

## Product Principles

1. Every page is itself evidence of design skill, not just a description of it.
2. Optimize for a fast, skimmable first pass — recruiters are scanning, not reading.
3. Real project work takes priority placement over placeholder work; placeholders stay honest about their state.
4. Every path through the site should make it easy to reach the "get in touch / request an interview" action.
